# Cover Letter

Dear Editors,

I submit **"The Great Flattening: More Words, Fewer Ideas in AI Research"** for consideration.

The paper provides the first large-scale measurement of how the 2022 LLM transition changed the diversity of AI research. Using 1.53 million arXiv abstracts and a difference-in-differences design with mathematics and physics as controls, I document a paradox: AI abstracts became lexically richer and longer, but their meaning converged. Mean semantic similarity and the effective number of semantic modes (Vendi) moved toward homogeneity, and the estimates survive field-specific trends, a length-standardized replication on truncated abstracts, and pre-trend checks. Within-author comparisons show that the same researchers moved toward the new equilibrium. The paper also reports where the evidence is weaker: the within-topic estimate is sensitive to pre-trends, and the lexical and topic-concentration results do not survive all robustness checks.

The results have implications for science policy, peer review, and the evaluation of AI systems. If the scientific record becomes more semantically homogeneous, benchmarks and leaderboards may overstate progress because models are trained and evaluated on an increasingly narrow distribution of problems and styles.

The paper is independent research. All code and derived data are available at https://github.com/MohammadHamidReza/great-flattening. The author declares no competing interests.
