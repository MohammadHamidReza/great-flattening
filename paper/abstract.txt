Title: The Great Flattening: More Words, Fewer Ideas in AI Research

Author: Mohammad Hamid Reza, Independent Researcher
Email: mohammadhamidreza7@gmail.com
Date: November 2026

Abstract:
Scientific progress depends on conceptual diversity, not just output volume. Large language models (LLMs) are now embedded in scientific writing, but evidence on whether they narrow that diversity comes from small samples or creative tasks. I analyze 1,525,557 arXiv abstracts (2010-2026), with a stratified sample of 139,158 papers for semantic measures, across AI, mathematics, and physics. Contrary to the lexical homogenization hypothesis, AI abstracts became lexically more diverse and longer after 2022. But their semantic structure converged: mean pairwise cosine similarity rose by 17.5% in AI fields versus 2.7% in controls; within-topic similarity rose by 10.1% versus 0.7%; and the effective number of semantic modes (Vendi) fell by 14.1% versus 1.1%. The mean-similarity and Vendi estimates survive field-specific trends, a length-standardized replication on truncated abstracts, and pre-trend checks; the within-topic estimate is supportive but sensitive to pre-trends. LLM-characteristic phrases rose sharply, and within-author comparisons show that the same researchers became more self-similar after 2023. The pattern is strongest in NLP, weaker in ML/AI, and largely absent in mathematics and physics. AI research is producing more words but fewer distinct semantic modes-a great flattening that may narrow the scientific frontier.

Keywords: AI and science; semantic homogenization; LLM-assisted writing; science of science; arXiv; evaluation.
